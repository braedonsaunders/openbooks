# BOJ/MoF selling and September 2026 equity risk

**As of September 8, 2026 (about 2:10 p.m. ET).** This is a risk framework, not individualized tax or investment advice. Probabilities below are judgment ranges, not model outputs.

## Verdict: true in a narrow sense, overstated in the claim

The accurate headline is: **Japan's Ministry of Finance (MoF) conducted a record-size yen-support intervention and August reserve assets posted a record monthly decline.** “The BOJ is selling at its fastest rate ever” conflates three different things:

1. **FX intervention — record:** MoF reports ¥15.3993 trillion of intervention from July 30 through August 26. Contemporary reporting identifies it as dollar-selling/yen-buying, including coordinated U.S.-Japan action on July 31. This was the largest monthly yen-buying intervention amount, and 2026 intervention has also surpassed the prior annual record.
2. **Reserve drawdown — record, but not all sales:** Official reserve assets fell $79.575 billion (6.18%) in August to $1.2075 trillion, the largest monthly dollar and percentage decline in the series beginning in 2000. The narrower foreign-currency-reserve bucket fell more—about $94.6 billion to $995.0 billion—while higher gold value partly offset that decline in total reserves.
3. **BOJ balance-sheet policy — not record selling:** The MoF decides intervention and owns the Foreign Exchange Fund Special Account; the BOJ executes as MoF's agent. Separately, the BOJ is still **buying** JGBs, though less each quarter. Its June plan calls for about ¥2.5 trillion per month in July–September, ¥2.3 trillion in October–December, and ¥2.0 trillion from April 2027. Holdings decline mainly because purchases are below redemptions, not because the BOJ is dumping JGBs.

Foreign-currency securities fell about $87.8 billion, or 9.5%, to $839.6 billion, while deposits fell about $6.9 billion to $155.4 billion. The close match between the securities decline and intervention size makes substantial security sales—likely including U.S. Treasurys—the leading explanation. It is **not proof of $87.8 billion in Treasury sales**: MoF does not disclose security type or maturity in this release, securities are marked to market, and non-dollar assets use current exchange rates. MoF explicitly cited intervention plus bond valuation losses as yields rose. Bloomberg noted that the 10-year Treasury's small month-end price change implies valuation was probably only a small part of the securities decline, but “probably” remains the right word.

Japan has not announced a continuing daily sale rate. August is a backward-looking stock snapshot of an unusually large, partly coordinated intervention. MoF also says it plans to use the Fed's FIMA repo facility in future. FIMA can temporarily exchange Treasurys for dollars rather than force an immediate market sale; it can smooth Treasury-market impact, but it is short-term, collateralized backstop funding—not free permanent intervention capacity. Further intervention can therefore occur without contemporaneous Treasury sales, although persistent intervention ultimately still requires durable funding.

**Bottom line:** “fastest-ever Japanese yen intervention/reserve drawdown” is supported. “BOJ selling fastest ever” and “therefore September equities must fall” are not.

## Transmission to U.S. equities and a CAD-denominated TFSA

The risk is real but conditional, with four main channels:

- **U.S. yields:** Outright Treasury liquidation adds supply and can lift term premium. Higher real/nominal yields compress long-duration equity multiples. TEC and high-growth CRDO/RKLB are more rate-sensitive than diversified XEQT; FBTC is vulnerable through liquidity and risk appetite rather than discounted cash flow.
- **Yen carry unwind:** A fast yen rally raises the cost of yen-funded positions and can force leveraged selling across equities, credit and crypto. On September 8 Reuters reported the yen up about 4.5% in a week and a seven-month high. This current carry unwind, not the reserve-data headline by itself, is the more immediate cross-asset signal.
- **Japanese repatriation:** Higher JGB yields can make domestic assets more attractive to Japanese investors. Repatriation is gradual unless volatility and hedging costs jump; it is not equivalent to MoF selling reserves.
- **Currency translation into CAD:** In a conventional risk-off move, USD often strengthens against CAD, cushioning unhedged U.S. holdings when measured in CAD. A stronger yen can help XEQT's unhedged Japanese exposure in CAD even while hurting Japanese exporters. These offsets are unstable in a disorderly deleveraging.

For the TFSA, there is no capital-loss deduction to harvest. Trading inside the account does not itself remove contribution room, but a withdrawal is restored only the next calendar year. That favours trimming concentrated risk before disrupting a diversified core and avoiding unnecessary churn. The proposed sale order should therefore protect XEQT and the stated long-term SPCX holding; TEC is a hold unless the broader stress regime is confirmed; FBTC remains no-add; PATH buys remain paused.

## Ladder decision: keep levels; tighten definitions and actions

Do **not** lower thresholds solely because of the reserve headline. Keep the existing market-based levels, because they observe transmission rather than assume it:

- **Yellow:** VIX ≥20 **or** SPY −3% **or** QQQ −5% from the rolling 20-trading-day high.
- **Orange:** VIX ≥25 **or** SPY −5% from the rolling 20-trading-day high.
- **Red:** VIX ≥30 **or** SPY −8% from the rolling 20-trading-day high **or** SPY falls 3% in one session.

Tighten implementation as follows:

1. Fix one data source, use adjusted closing prices for rolling-high drawdowns, recalculate daily, and log the reference high. Use VIX's official close. Yellow may be flagged intraday, but act from the close.
2. **Yellow = freeze, not sell:** no FBTC/PATH additions, no averaging into CRDO/RKLB, and review rates/FX/credit after the close.
3. **Orange = first trim:** if confirmed at the close, trim a preselected 10–15% of each CRDO/RKLB position. If only VIX triggers while SPY/QQQ and credit remain orderly, use the low end rather than treating a volatility spike as a crash.
4. **Red = second-stage defence:** trim another preselected 20–25% of remaining CRDO/RKLB. Review a modest TEC trim only if Red persists into a second close or Treasury/carry stress is clearly disorderly. XEQT and SPCX are last, not first, sources of cash.
5. Cancel the escalation if the triggering index closes back above its threshold and VIX closes below the relevant band; do not automatically rebuy until the next scheduled review.

At the current published 20-day highs, rough first reference levels are SPY $756.0 for Yellow, $740.4 for Orange and $717.0 for Red; QQQ Yellow is about $697.9. These are monitoring aids, not permanent limit prices—rolling highs and distributions change them.

## What would falsify the bearish interpretation?

Treat the “BOJ selling causes September downturn” thesis as falsified or materially weakened if most of the following occur:

- MoF reports no new intervention and September reserves/securities stabilize, or disclosed FIMA/deposit funding clearly substitutes for near-term Treasury liquidation.
- U.S. Treasury yields, auction tails and bid/ask liquidity remain orderly despite the August reserve data.
- USD/JPY stabilizes, the yen carry unwind loses momentum, and cross-asset leverage stress does not spread into credit.
- The BOJ delivers the expected 25 bp hike but guidance is not more hawkish than the path already priced; Fed pricing absorbs CPI without a disorderly yield jump.
- VIX remains below 20 on closes, breadth improves, and SPY/QQQ reclaim their rolling highs rather than triggering Yellow.

Conversely, the thesis gains support only with **new evidence**: fresh large intervention, another outsized securities drawdown, rising Treasury term premium/liquidity stress, accelerating yen gains and confirmed equity/VIX ladder breaches. A recycled August headline is not new evidence.
